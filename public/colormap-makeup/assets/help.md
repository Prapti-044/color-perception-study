# Help - Color Perception Study Guide

## Study Overview

Welcome to our color perception research study! This experiment investigates how individuals perceive and interpret colors across various visual stimuli. This is a replication of Danielle Albers Szafir's research on "Modeling Color Difference for Visualization Design."

## What to Expect

The study consists of several phases designed to systematically analyze your color perception abilities:

### Phase 1: Setup and Training
- **Consent Form**: Review and agree to participate in the study
- **Introduction**: Learn about the study objectives and procedures
- **Basic Information**: Provide your name and confirm you don't have color vision deficiency
- **Color Training**: Complete 3 practice tasks to familiarize yourself with color comparison questions

### Phase 2: Color Perception Tasks
You'll view multiple scatterplot visualizations and answer whether two colored points appear the same or different. The study includes:

- Main scatterplot comparisons with varying color differences
- Control tasks with identical colors (should appear "Same")
- Large difference tasks with obviously different colors
- Color reset screens to neutralize your eye's color adaptation between sections

### Phase 3: Attention Checks
Throughout the study, you'll encounter simple attention check questions (like counting letters in words) to ensure you're paying attention.

### Phase 4: Background Questionnaires
- **Demographics**: Age, gender, education, etc.
- **Color Theory Knowledge**: Questions about your experience with color theory
- **Makeup Experience**: Questions about your familiarity and use of makeup products

## How to Respond

### For Color Comparison Tasks:
- Look carefully at the two colored points in each scatterplot
- Decide whether they appear to be the **same color** or **different colors**
- Select your answer using the radio buttons in the sidebar
- There are no "trick" questions - trust your visual perception
- Click **Next** to proceed to the next task

### For Questionnaires:
- Read each question carefully
- Provide honest, thoughtful responses
- Some questions are required and must be answered to continue
- Use the text boxes for open-ended responses when prompted

## Important Guidelines

### Technical Requirements:
- Use a computer with a good quality monitor
- Ensure adequate lighting in your workspace
- Avoid glare or reflections on your screen
- Complete the study in one sitting (approximately 30-45 minutes)

### Study Integrity:
- Answer based on your own perception - there are no "right" or "wrong" answers for most tasks
- Don't use external tools or get help from others
- Some tasks may seem repetitive - this is intentional for research purposes
- Pay attention to attention check questions throughout

### Navigation:
- Use the **Next** button to advance through tasks
- Progress is shown in the progress bar at the top
- The sidebar shows your current task and response options
- You cannot go back to previous questions once submitted

## Getting Help

- **Technical Issues**: If you encounter technical problems, refresh your browser
- **Questions**: Contact the researcher at u1452119@utah.edu
- **Study Purpose**: Detailed information about the study's purpose will be provided at the end

## Data Privacy

- All responses are confidential and reported only in aggregate
- No personally identifying information is collected beyond what you choose to provide
- Your participation is voluntary and you may withdraw at any time

## Tips for Success

1. **Take your time** - There's no time pressure for individual questions
2. **Trust your perception** - Go with your first impression of color differences
3. **Stay focused** - Minimize distractions during the study
4. **Be consistent** - Use the same criteria for "same" vs "different" throughout
5. **Rest your eyes** - The color reset screens help prevent eye fatigue

Thank you for participating in this important research on color perception!
